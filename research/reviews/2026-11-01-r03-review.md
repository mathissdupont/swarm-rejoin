## Sound parts worth keeping

1. Position covariance, observation age, and clock integrity are treated separately. Navigation evidence is not confused with software authorization or device attestation.
2. A low consistency statistic is not presented as proof of an attack-free vehicle; a high statistic is not presented as a unique spoofing diagnosis.
3. Missing GNSS consistency evidence produces UNKNOWN, rather than silently passing a test that could not run.
4. Ground truth is separate from the estimator and policy inputs. Step/ramp injections are correctly labeled fault abstractions rather than complete RF spoofing simulations.
5. The proposed thresholds and sensitivity grid are explicitly distinguished from sensor specifications and measured performance.

## Reproducible numerical check

Environment used: Python 3.14.2, SciPy 1.18.1. PDF text/identity checks used pypdf 6.11.0. These are review-tool versions, not project runtime requirements; no new mandatory dependency was added.

```python
from scipy.stats import chi2

for alpha in (0.05, 0.01, 0.001):
    print(alpha, round(chi2.isf(alpha, 3), 3), round(chi2.isf(alpha, 6), 3))
```

```text
0.05  7.815  12.592
0.01  11.345 16.812
0.001 16.266 22.458
```

See the [official SciPy distribution API](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.chi2.html) for inverse-survival semantics. The report's 36 expected exceedances per hour example is consistent with 3,600 opportunities each having marginal probability 0.01; independence is not needed for the expected count. Probability of any exceedance, alarm episodes, and latched alarms are different quantities. This check establishes none of the Gaussian/calibration assumptions on UAV data.
