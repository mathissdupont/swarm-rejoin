# Abstract-level note: Notomista et al. (2021 preprint)

- Source: `notomista2021`, [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05586v1), [rights/access record](../../library/notomista2021/README.md).
- Checked: 2026-11-27. Original abstract, arXiv license link and Crossref bibliographic metadata only; no full-method review or replication.
- Version distinction: v1 is dated 12 May 2021; Crossref records the journal article in February 2022, DOI [10.1109/TRO.2021.3102379](https://doi.org/10.1109/TRO.2021.3102379), with “Multirobot” in the journal title.

The abstract explicitly models robot features and capabilities, environmental changes and component failures for resilient allocation. Consequently, capability-aware resilience is established nearby work; the proposed recovery-aware update study cannot claim that general idea as new.

The full constraints, recourse/failure model and ability to express software authorization and navigation-readiness transitions remain unexamined. A generic branch-aware comparator may subsume our candidate. Do not interpret the abstract's silence about updates as proof of missing coverage.

The arXiv nonexclusive distribution grant is to arXiv. No independent repository republication permission was established, so this source remains record-only. Next: examine the original allocation model and whether its existing capability mapping can represent our transition branches without a new method.
