## Question, method and assumptions

The study asks how a distributed UAV cluster can re-admit a disconnected member and preserve task results across disconnections. The cluster head appraises the candidate's identity, program and performance on a limited protocol-compliance task. Membership and key information are recorded in the IDBC. The system overview limits its disconnection handling to one UAV disconnection at a time within the same cluster. Its reference program and trusted-enforcement assumptions must be matched before using its full protocol as a comparator.

## Relevance and evaluation boundary

The strongest overlap is the use of identity, program integrity and observed behavior to govern re-admission. The GPS command example makes a claim that Dong omits GPS untenable. The concrete question left for comparison is whether evidence age and quantitative navigation readiness support a different role decision under otherwise equivalent software and admission conditions. The supplied evaluation example does not measure that outcome. The paper's simulations and formal claims have not been reproduced here.

For an assumption-matched test, keep software authorization, recovery options and the evidence available to both policies fixed. Use a common admission rule that retains the established identity, program and task-compliance checks. Compare the bounded recovery/navigation contract against the competent baseline defined by the project and assess useful role service and unsafe grants against independent scenario truth. This is a proposed comparison. Another author should check the cited sections, inspect the remaining assumptions and record their review before R01 closure.
